A B S T R A C T

[BLOCK b0007]
This study systematically investigates the tension–tension fatigue behavior and deformation mechanisms of solution-treated and aging-treated Fe-30.5Mn-8Al-1C (wt%) austenitic low-density steels. focusing on the critical role of κ-carbide precipitation state in controlling fatigue properties. In aged samples, intragranular κ-carbides induce planar dislocation slip through a “glide plane softening” mechanism, enhancing slip reversibility under cyclic loading and thereby improving fatigue life. Strengthening is primarily due to the interaction between dislocations and intragranular k-carbides. An appropriate increase in the size of intragranular κ-carbides significantly enhances fatigue life and fatigue strength at low stress amplitudes. Conversely, intergranular κ-carbide precipitation impedes slip transmission, intensifies localized stress concentration, and accelerates damage, thus reducing fatigue life at high stress amplitudes. These findings strongly demonstrate that acceler ating the precipitation of intragranular κ-carbides while suppressing intergranular precipitation is an effective microstructural pathway to concurrently enhance fatigue performance of Fe-Mn-Al-C austenitic low-density steels across the entire range of stress amplitudes

[BLOCK b0008]
1. Introduction

[BLOCK b0009]
Driven by increasingly stringent emission regulations and fuel effi ciency demands, the pursuit of automotive lightweighting has intensi fied the search for materials with superior strength-to-weight ratios. Low-density steels, especially Fe-Mn-Al-C alloys, have emerged as promising candidates [1–5]. The addition of \~10 wt% aluminum re duces density by approximately 10–13 % compared with conventional steels, while maintaining or even enhancing strength–ductility synergy [3,6–8]. Some previous studies have shown that optimized aging treatment facilitates the uniform precipitation of high-density nanoscale (Fe,Mn) AlC κ-carbides within the γ matrix through spinodal decom position [9,10]. These intragranular κ-carbides provide pronounced precipitation hardening [11–15]. However, high-temperature exposure also triggers discontinuous precipitation and cellular transformation at grain boundaries (GBs), leading to the formation of coarse intergranular κ-carbides [16,17]. Such precipitates would concentrate interfacial stress, degrade mechanical properties, and significantly compromise alloy reliability [2,18]. Accordingly, tailoring κ-carbide precipitation to achieve intragranular refinement while suppressing intergranular pre cipitate coarsening is of critical importance for the performance

[BLOCK b0010]
optimization.

[BLOCK b0011]
Fatigue failure constitutes an important failure mode in automotive structures and components [19,20]. As vehicle weight is reduced, ensuring adequate fatigue life and maintaining high strength and ductility becomes equally critical for safety and reliability. Consequently, understanding the fatigue performance of advanced lowdensity steels is of considerable engineering significance. Gomez et al. [21] reported that intragranular κ-carbides provide precipitation strengthening through dislocation shearing, significantly improving the high-cycle fatigue (HCF) performance in Fe-29Mn-8.7Al-1C. Wegener et al. [22] conducted a low-cycle fatigue (LCF) study on Fe-29.8Mn 7.65Al-1.11C steel, and found that intragranular κ-carbides improved fatigue life at a low strain amplitude but reduced fatigue life at a high strain amplitude due to aggravated cyclic softening. Li et al. [23] per formed tension–compression fatigue tests on solution-treated Fe-26Mn-10Al-C alloys, observing rapid initial cyclic softening, followed by cyclic hardening and eventual performance degradation. They attributed the hardening to the dynamic precipitation of κ-carbides during cyclic deformation. Currently, investigations on the fatigue behavior of Fe-Mn-Al-C austenitic steels still remain relatively limited, primarily focusing on the influence of intragranular κ-carbides. However, the mechanisms by which different intragranular κ-carbide precipitation states affect fatigue performance are still poorly understood, particularly regarding the role of intergranular κ-carbides.

[BLOCK b0012]
The present study systematically investigates the fatigue behavior of an Fe-30.5Mn-8Al-1C (wt%) alloy under tension–tension cyclic loading. By tailoring the morphology and distribution of κ-carbide precipitation through controlled thermal treatments, the effects of both intragranular and intergranular κ-carbides on the fatigue life, cyclic response, and damage mechanisms are elucidated. This work is expected to provide fundamental insights crucial for the development of fatigue-resistant, high-strength, and low-density steels that are suitable for automotive structural applications under severe cyclic loading conditions.

[BLOCK b0013]
2. Experimental materials and methods

[BLOCK b0014]
2.1. Materials

[BLOCK b0015]
The investigated steel has a nominal composition of Fe-30.5Mn-8Al 1C (wt%). A 50 kg ingot was prepared by vacuum melting and subse quently forged into a billet with a cross-section of 30 mm × 30 mm. The billet was then hot-rolled to a final thickness of 4 mm. Solution treat ment was conducted at 1050 <sup>◦</sup>C for 1 h, followed by water quenching to room temperature; this condition is referred to as ST. To obtain different precipitation states of intragranular and intergranular κ-carbides, selected ST samples were further aged at 550 <sup>◦</sup>C, 600 <sup>◦</sup>C, and 650 <sup>◦</sup>C for 6 h, designated as 550-6, 600-6, and 650-6, respectively.

[BLOCK b0016]
2.2. Mechanical tests

[BLOCK b0017]
Flat dog-bone-shaped tensile samples were machined from the sheets using wire electrical discharge machining (EDM), with their tensile axes parallel to the rolling direction. A schematic of the shape and di mensions of the tensile test samples is provided in Fig. 1. The sample surfaces were mechanically polished and subsequently electro-polished in a solution of 8 vol.% perchloric acid in ethanol for 30 s at 25 V to remove surface stresses and scratches. Uniaxially tensile tests were performed at room temperature using a universal testing machine (Shimadzu AG-Xplus 250 kN) under a constant strain rate of $1 0 ^ { - 3 } s ^ { - 1 }$

[BLOCK b0018]
Flat dog-bone-shaped fatigue samples were also extracted from the sheets via EDM, with the loading axis parallel to the rolling direction. These samples shared the same dimensions as the tensile samples (Fig. 1). The surfaces were prepared using the same procedure of me chanical grinding and electropolishing to ensure a stress-free and smooth finish. Stress-controlled tension–tension fatigue tests were con ducted in ambient air using a servo-hydraulic fatigue testing machine (Shimadzu EHF-EV050K2A-020-1). A sinusoidal waveform was adopted in fatigue tests at a frequency of 20 Hz and a stress ratio of R = 0.1.

[BLOCK b0019]
2.3. Microstructural characterizations

[BLOCK b0020]
The microstructure of the undeformed ST sample was characterized by electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) on a TESCAN MIRA3 scan ning electron microscope (SEM) equipped with an Oxford EBSD detec tor. Prior to observations, the sample surface was prepared through mechanical polishing followed by electropolishing. Transmission elec tron microscopy (TEM) was conducted on a JEOL JEM-2100F micro scope operating at 200 kV to characterize the precipitation of κ-carbides in the aged samples (550-6, 600-6 and 650-6). TEM foils were prepared by mechanically grinding slices to approximately 50 μm in thickness, followed by twin-jet electropolishing in a solution of 8 vol.% perchloric acid in ethanol at 30 V. The electrolyte was maintained at approximately − 20 <sup>◦</sup>C to prevent sample oxidation.

[BLOCK b0021]
For the post-fractured samples, the fracture surfaces and lateral surfaces were examined using a JEOL JSM-7800F SEM. Furthermore, EBSD was employed to analyze crack propagation path on externa surfaces. These scans were acquired using a step size of 0.8 μm, and the data were analyzed using AZtecCrystal post-processing software. To gain further insight into the deformation mechanisms, the deformation microstructures in the post-fractured samples were characterized by TEM. TEM foils were extracted from the gauge sections of the deformed specimens to ensure microstructural representativeness.

3.1. Microstructures prior to deformation

[BLOCK b0024]
Fig. 2 shows the microstructures in the ST and aged samples. The inverse pole figure (IPF) map of the ST sample reveals a fully austenitic microstructure (Fig. 2a). After aging, the 550-6 sample exhibits a high density of nanoscale κ-carbides (\~11 nm) with rectangular morphology, uniformly precipitated within the austenite grains (Fig. 2b). With increasing aging temperature, the intragranular κ-carbides in the 600-6 sample coarsen to \~18 nm, and evolve into larger rectangular paral lelepipeds to form well-defined arrays aligned along the orthogonal <100> directions of the matrix (Fig. 2c). Concurrently, intergranular κ-carbide precipitation is also observable. Further increasing aging temperature to 650 <sup>◦</sup>C leads to coarsening of intragranular κ-carbides to approximately 24 nm, accompanied by more pronounced intergranular precipitation in the 650-6 sample (Fig. 2d). The size of intragranular κ-carbides is quantified using an equivalent-circle diameter, and the quantitative evolution of κ-carbide size with aging temperature is summarized in Fig. 3. The detailed κ-carbide precipitation behavior of the Fe-30.5Mn-8Al-1C alloy during aging treatment has been reported in our previous work [6]. This microstructural overview provides the basis for interpreting the subsequent fatigue behavior.

3.4. Fracture characteristics

[BLOCK b0041]
Fig. 6 displays the macroscopic fatigue fracture surfaces of the Fe-30.5Mn-8Al-1C steel tested at stress amplitudes of 180 and 315 MPa. All samples exhibit a similar three-zone fracture morphology, comprising the crack initiation, propagation, and rapid fracture regions, with cracks initiating at the surface and propagating radially inward. At a given stress amplitude, the area fraction (fa) of the crack initiation and propagation zones (marked by yellow dashed lines in Fig. 6) is larger in the aged samples than in the ST sample; it is consistent with their extended fatigue life. The corresponding fa values were quantitatively measured and are labeled in the upper-right corner of each subfigure. Specifically, the 600-6 sample showed the largest area fraction at 180 MPa, while the 550-6 sample exhibited the highest at 315 MPa, in line with their respective optimal fatigue resistance at low and high stress amplitudes.

[BLOCK b0042]
Fig. 7 illustrates high-magnification views of the fatigue crack propagation zone in the ST, 550-6, 600-6, and 650-6 samples tested at two stress amplitudes of 180 and 315 MPa. Well-defined fatigue stria tions were observed in all samples, indicating transgranular crack advancement via reversible dislocation slip on specific crystallographic planes [27]. The formation of fatigue striations typically corresponds to cyclic crack tip blunting during loading and re-sharpening during unloading, and a reduced striation spacing indicates a lower crack propagation rate [28–30]. Notably, the 550-6 and 600-6 samples exhibit more pronounced and closely spaced striations, with the narrowest spacing observed in the 550-6 sample at 315 MPa and in the 600-6 sample at 180 MPa. This corresponds to their respective longer fatigue lives shown in Fig. 5.

[BLOCK b0043]
At the final fracture stage, the rapid fracture zones of the ST, 550-6, and 600-6 samples are predominantly characterized by dimples (Fig. 8a-$\mathrm { c , e { - } g ) , }$ , indicating a microvoid coalescence mechanism and ductile fail ure. In contrast, the 650-6 sample displays mixed fracture characteristics (Fig. 8d,h), featuring both dimples and cleavage faceted regions. These facets are typically associated with reduced ductility, which is primarily attributed to the extensive precipitation of intergranular κ-carbides in the 650-6 sample.

3.5. Crack propagation behavior

[BLOCK b0045]
To elucidate the crack-path characteristics of the fatigued samples, EBSD-based band contrast (BC) and IPF superimposed maps, Kernel average misorientation (KAM) maps, and Schmid factor (SF) maps were acquired adjacent to the main cracks at a stress amplitude of 225 MPa (Fig. 9). The BC and IPF superimposed images $( \mathrm { F i g . ~ } 9 \mathrm { a } , \mathrm { d } , \mathrm { g } , \mathrm { j } )$ reveal that cracks in all four samples propagate along slip bands in a transgranular mode; it is consistent with crack advance along favorably oriented {111} slip planes in face-centered cubic (FCC) alloys [31–34]. Despite this common fracture mode, clear differences in path geometry are evident. In the ST sample, the crack path within large austenite grains is rela tively straight. In contrast, the aged samples (550-6, 600-6, and 650-6) exhibit significantly greater path tortuosity and frequent microbranching as the crack interacts with the heterogeneous slip field. It should be noted, however, that the κ-carbides are nanoscale precipitates, whereas the fatigue crack path resolved by EBSD is on the micrometer scale. Given the step size and spatial resolution emploved. individual intragranular κ-carbides cannot be resolved in the EBSD maps, making it impossible to establish a one-to-one correspondence between single precipitates and local crack deflections. Instead, the influence of κ-car bide precipitation is reflected at a more global level through its effect on the dominant slip mode, the degree of strain localization, and the resulting crack-path morphology. Based on the increased branching and deflection observed in the aged samples, we infer that the intragranular κ-carbides act as effective nanoscale obstacles that impede dislocation movement and redistribute the local crack-tip stress field. This process promotes the activation of multiple slip systems, thereby inducing crack deflection or branching and enhancing crack propagation resistance [35].

3.7. Deformation microstructures

[BLOCK b0060]
Fig. 11 shows the typical dislocation structures in the ST and aged samples (550-6, 600-6, 650-6) fatigued at Δσ/2 of 180 and 315 MPa. Planar dislocation slip was observed to be the dominant deformation mechanism in all cases. At Δσ/2 = 180 MPa, the ST sample exhibits well developed dislocation bands composed of dense dislocation arrays aligned along primary slip planes (Fig. 11a), namely persistent Lüder’s bands (PLBs), which are indicative of strain localization in fatigued samples. In contrast, the aged samples exhibit a substantially lower overall dislocation density and more uniformly distributed planar slip bands (Fig. 11c,e,g). This difference arises from the fine intragranular κ-carbides, which dynamically interact with gliding dislocations during cycling, impede their slip and accumulation, and thereby suppress the formation and coalescence of localized bands. At the higher stress amplitude of $\Delta \sigma / 2 \ : = \ : 3 1 5$ MPa (Fig. 11b,d,f,h), all samples exhibit similar features to those at $\Delta \sigma / 2 = 1 8 0 ~ \mathrm { M P a } .$ , but with narrower slip band spacing and more extensive dislocation movement. The ST sample shows more intensely localized bands, whereas the aged samples maintain a comparatively uniform network of planar bands. These microstructural observations align with the improved fatigue resistance of the aged samples, particularly 600-6, by limiting strain localization and delaying damage evolution.

4. Discussion

[BLOCK b0062]
4.1. Impact of intragranular κ-carbides on dislocation slip mode

[BLOCK b0063]
In the ST sample, the observed planar slip bands are primarily attributed to the short-range ordering (SRO) structure within the austenitic matrix. This observation aligns with the “glide plane soft ening” effect proposed by Gerold and Karnthaler [40]. The strong C-Mn chemical affinity leads to a non-random solute distribution, forming locally ordered clusters [41–43]. When a leading dislocation cuts through such an SRO region, it disrupts the local order and reduces the resistance on that specific glide plane, thereby facilitating subsequent coplanar glide and the formation of distinct planar slip bands [44–46]. However, because SRO structure is relatively weak and spatially het erogeneous, repeated shearing by moving dislocations tends to destroy it prematurely. The resulting non-uniform softening across potential slip planes fosters local strain concentrations (Fig. 12a).

[BLOCK b0064]
Aging treatment induces coherent ordered L1 -structured intra granular κ-carbides that amplify “glide plane softening” effect beyond the SRO level. When moving dislocations shear these precipitates [47], anti-phase boundaries (APBs) are created. After a plane has been swept and order locally disrupted, subsequent dislocations traverse the same plane with reduced resistance because additional APBs are no longer formed [42]. This path-dependent process channels dislocation activity to a limited set of previously softened glide planes, strengthening slip planarity and suppressing cross-slip. The uniform nanoscale dispersion of κ-carbides promotes the homogeneous formation of planar slip bands and distributes plastic strain over multiple coplanar systems, thereby mitigating localized strain concentrations. Crucially, this enhanced slip planarity and restricted cross-slip significantly improve the reversibility of dislocation motion under cyclic loading [44]. Dislocations can more readily retrace their path on well-defined softened planes during load reversal, leading to reduced accumulated damage per cycle (Fig. 12b). These mechanisms rationalize the superior fatigue performance of the aged samples relative to the ST samples.

[BLOCK b0065]
4.2. Effect of intragranular κ-carbides on fatigue behavior at low stress amplitudes

[BLOCK b0066]
At low stress amplitudes, the fatigue crack initiation stage occupies a significant portion of the total fatigue life; strengthening the matrix is therefore an effective route to delay initiation [48,49]. In Fe-Mn-Al-C alloys, precipitation strengthening by intragranular κ-carbides is domi nant, and the active dislocation-precipitate interaction depends on the size of precipitates. The dominant mechanism transitions from shearing to Orowan bypassing as the precipitate size increases, and a critical size of approximately 27 nm was reported in analogous alloy systems [50]. In the present work, intragranular κ-carbides in both the 550-6 and 650- 6 samples (with average sizes of \~11 nm and \~24 nm, respectively) remain in the shearing regime (Fig. 13a,b). Fig. 13(c,d) present high resolution TEM (HRTEM) images of κ-carbides in the fatigued 550-6 and 650-6 samples, respectively. The presence of shear steps and localized contrast variations within the carbides (indicated by red arrows) suggests a disruption of the ordered structure and the formation of APBs, which is consistent with the view that repeated dislocation shearing causes me chanical dissolution of κ-carbides during cyclic loading, as also reported by Wegener et al. [22].

4.3. Effect of intergranular κ-carbides on fatigue behavior at high stress amplitudes

[BLOCK b0076]
At high stress amplitudes, the activation of mobile dislocations be comes more pronounced, and their interactions with grain boundaries are more significant. Thus, the state of grain boundaries exerts a strong influence on fatigue life [51]. Plastic deformation heterogeneity in metallic materials predominantly arises from SFs mismatch between adjacent grains. “Soft-oriented” grains (high SF) can activate dislocation slip at lower applied stress compared with “hard-oriented” grains (low SF). According to Schmid’s law, the critical resolved shear stress (τ ) required for slip system activation in FCC metals satisfies:

[BLOCK b0077]
$$
\sigma_ {\mathrm{a}} = \tau_ {\mathrm{c}} / \mu\tag{5}
$$

[BLOCK b0078]
where $\mu$ is the Schmid factor and the $\sigma _ { \mathrm { a } }$ is the applied tensile stress. During cyclic deformation, dislocations are preferentially multiplicated and glide in soft-oriented grains, leading to pile‑ups against boundaries shared with hard‑oriented neighbors.

[BLOCK b0079]
The dislocation distribution is clearly demonstrated by TEM char acterizations of microstructures adjacent to grain boundaries after fa tigue at a $\Delta \sigma / 2$ of 315 MPa (Fig. 14). In the 550-6 sample (Fig. 14a), κ-carbides are uniformly distributed within the grains, with no coarse precipitation at the grain boundaries. These intragranular κ-carbides facilitate organized planar slip bands, promoting relatively uniform slip transfer across grain boundaries [26]. By contrast, the 650-6 sample exhibits coarse intergranular κ-carbide precipitation disrupting the continuity of grain boundaries (Fig. 14b). These brittle particles act as strong slip barriers, intensifying dislocation pile-ups, promoting the formation of tangled dislocation structures with wavy-slip patterns, elevating local stress concentration, and eventually leading to earlier crack nucleation and shortened fatigue life.

[BLOCK b0080]
Based on the above analyses, the influence of intergranular κ-car bides on the fatigue deformation mechanism is schematically illustrated in Fig. 15. These differences in slip behavior directly correlate with the fatigue performance at high stress amplitudes. In the 550-6 sample, which has a homogeneous intragranular κ-carbide distribution and an absence of intergranular κ-carbides, dislocation slip transfer is facili tated and localization is suppressed. In the 650-6 sample, the presence of severe intergranular κ-carbides impedes dislocation slip at grain boundaries to cause severe dislocation pile-ups, exacerbates local stress concentrations, and accelerates strain localization, leading to premature crack initiation and failure. Consistently, the fatigue life ranking at high amplitudes is 550-6 sample of best, 600-6 of intermediate, and 650-6 of poorest, tracking the increasing severity of intergranular precipitation. Apparently, such a detrimental grain boundary effect that offsets the size benefit at low stress amplitudes discussed in Section 4.2 also degrades fatigue resistance at high stress amplitudes by the same mechanism.

[BLOCK b0081]
Based on the above analyses, achieving an optimal κ-carbide pre cipitation state is essential for enhancing the fatigue performance of Fe-Mn-Al-C austenitic low-density steels. This requires a critical balance, namely, promoting a uniform distribution of intragranular κ-carbides with an appropriate size, while effectively suppressing detrimental precipitation at grain boundaries. Beneficial intragranular precipitates improve fatigue strength and fatigue life at low stress amplitudes by enhancing precipitation strengthening and dislocation slip reversibility. In contrast, coarse intergranular κ-carbides significantly degrade fatigue life at high stress amplitudes by accelerating stress concentration. Thus, the superior overall fatigue properties can be effectively achieved through microstructural control that maximizes the advantages of intragranular precipitation and simultaneously minimizes the harmful effects of intergranular precipitation.

[BLOCK b0082]
5. Conclusions

[BLOCK b0083]
This study systematically investigated the tension–tension fatigue behavior of an Fe-30.5Mn-8Al-1C low-density steel in both ST and aged conditions, focusing on the critical role of κ-carbides in governing fa tigue performance, and the main conclusions can be drawn as follows.

[BLOCK b0084]
(1) In both ST and aged conditions, planar dislocation slip occurs during cyclic deformation due to the “glide plane softening” ef fect. In ST, SRO-driven softening is weak and spatially hetero geneous, promoting cross-slip, to a certain extent, and local strain concentration. By contrast, uniformly dispersed intragranular κ-carbides enhance the “glide plane softening” effect through repeated shearing by moving dislocations, suppress cross-slip, increase the reversibility of dislocation motion, and conse quently improve fatigue life.
